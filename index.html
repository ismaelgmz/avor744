Early-stage engineering project · Mexico

Better cold storage for avocado.

AvoR744 is developing an engineering design for a stationary cold-storage chamber for avocado, using CO₂ (R-744) as a natural refrigerant.

The problem

Protecting quality after harvest.

Avocado continues to respire and mature after harvest. The project focuses on engineering a cold-storage system that can maintain the conditions needed for post-harvest conservation while considering the environmental impact of the refrigerant.

What we are designing

A refrigeration system sized from the chamber's total thermal load, followed by analysis of the CO₂ refrigeration cycle and selection of the main mechanical components from commercial technical catalogs.

The study also includes a life-cycle cost evaluation and comparison with equivalent systems using synthetic refrigerants.

Engineering approach

From thermal load to system selection.

01 · Thermal load

Transmission through walls, floor and roof, air infiltration, product load and internal loads.

02 · CO₂ cycle

Analysis of the refrigeration cycle using R-744, including the challenges associated with transcritical operation.

03 · Components

Mechanical equipment selection based on calculated requirements and commercial technical catalogs.

Why CO₂

A natural refrigerant with a different engineering challenge.

R-744 has zero ozone depletion potential and a reference GWP of 1. Its critical temperature is 31.1 °C, making transcritical operation an important design consideration in warm conditions.

Design considerations

High operating pressures require components and piping to be selected for the actual pressure conditions.

The project evaluates system performance and life-cycle economics rather than treating refrigerant selection as an isolated decision.

Team

Engineering students building the concept.
